# Risks

As of: end of M0 (planning). This page lists the product risks: what endangers the build, the
operations or the commitments of the product, how likely that is, what damage it does and how the
plan counters it. Individual threats with attacker, STRIDE category and tests are in the
[threat model](security/threat-model.md); here is only what remains of them as a risk for the
product. Risks of a single instance (own hosts, own accounts, own operations) are in the private
operations repository of that instance.

**Origin.** The risks of the team reports, of the core contract and of the follow-up on terminal and
visualization, merged: risks with the same cause share one row. Risks that a later decision
eliminated are under [Done](#done).

**Rating.** Likelihood and impact are each `low`, `medium` or `high`, as in the threat model
(section 6.1). If two reports rated the same risk differently, the higher rating applies, unless a
later decision reduced the cause; then the row names that decision.

**Maintenance.** At the end of every milestone the closing gate updates this page ([Plan](PLAN.md),
section 4 item 6): check the risks of the milestone, rate them again, move the done ones, add new
ones. The column "Check" names the milestone in which the risk is first confirmed or refuted with
the named test or ticket. IDs are never reassigned.

## The five biggest risks

| Rank | ID | Risk | Likelihood | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | RK-01 | Prompt injection steers agents with tools | high | high |
| 2 | RK-77 | With the first push the history becomes visible, including old sample values and internal identifiers | high | high |
| 3 | RK-13 | Wrong evidence of the reported mode in the reference adapter routes organization traffic through a personal subscription | medium | high |
| 4 | RK-23 | The host service on other people's hosts gives more than the permitted shell | medium | high |
| 5 | RK-07 | The memory of a runtime carries knowledge between audience groups | medium | high |

**Rationale for the order.** The order follows three criteria, in this order of precedence:

1. **Rating:** likelihood times impact from the register (`low` 1, `medium` 2,
   `high` 3).
2. **Kind of consequence:** The consequence leaves the instance or cannot be undone, that is, a
   later fix does not take it back: lockout by the provider, publication, data leak, content,
   knowledge or a shell in the hands of someone without permission for it, legal violation. A later
   fix remedies outages, breaks at interfaces, effort and restrictions; they do not meet the
   criterion.
3. **Remainder after the mitigation:** what no test in the repository can fully cover. If the
   [threat model](security/threat-model.md) lists the threat, its "Residual risk" level applies
   (with several, the highest). If it does not list it, the rationale below names a level on the
   same scale with a reason; it counts like a level of the threat model. At the same level, what
   counts is how far the remainder reaches.

`make docs` checks the first criterion with `tools/docs/risk_ranking.py` (D-W-78, D-W-79): the
table names the rating of the register, the rating never rises down the table, no risk outside the
table is rated higher than its last rank, and the list "Behind rank 5" names exactly the risks
outside the table with the same rating as rank 5. Criteria 2 and 3 are judgment calls; the
maintenance at the end of every milestone checks them.

1. **RK-01** and **RK-77** are the only risks with `high` and `high`. For both, the consequence
   leaves the instance and cannot be undone: a tool acts outside the instance, a published history
   cannot be taken back. RK-01 comes first because more remains: no model reliably detects injected
   instructions (T-AI-01 to T-AI-03, residual risk `medium`). The product therefore relies on
   boundaries instead of detection, and every new agent and every new tool widens the surface.
2. **RK-77** has a finite cause, the existing history. Two history scans
   (`make denylist-history`, gitleaks over all refs) find the places, only a commit without parents
   is published (D-W-93), and a red team checks the result. What
   remains are only identifiers that neither the deny list with its rules nor gitleaks detects. RK-77
   has no entry in the threat model; by criterion 3 this remainder is `low`: scans and
   red team check a cause that no longer grows, because after the switch
   `denylist-main.yml` checks every push to `main`.
3. **RK-13** leads the risks of the next rating (`medium` and `high` or `high` and
   `medium`). The consequence leaves the instance: the provider can lock out the whole organization,
   and that cannot be undone. An unknown reported mode aborts the run (fail closed).
   What remains is the case that the adapter reports a wrong mode as safe, because it depends on a
   non-public surface of Hermes Agent. No test in the repository fully covers that
   (T-AI-08, residual risk `medium` in the adapter), and this remainder hits the whole organization.
   Until D-W-57, RK-22 stood before RK-13 with the same consequence. Since ruling T12 every person on
   a host has their own session user, and since ruling T13 (D-W-62) no
   configured location connects the accounts again: working directories and `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` each
   belong to one session user (with `personal` it lies in the home, D-W-82), and HD11 checks every
   configured path on all three platforms (D-W-80). A subscription login then reaches
   no session of another person, even if `doctor` does not find it. The rationale of
   D-W-57 alone did not carry that: shared working directories were a second path (now
   RK-88). What remains depends on a local privilege escalation, on mistakes of the
   instance operator (a previously assigned name, a shared folder outside the configuration) or on
   a person's own action in such a folder. Therefore RK-22 is now at `low`.
4. **RK-23:** No fix takes back a shell or root-level privileges in the hands of someone without
   permission for it. After the mitigation, vulnerabilities in the operating system of the host
   remain (T-TM-04, residual risk `medium`); in addition there is a hard block: without proof for a
   platform there is no terminal there (M2-42). The remainder stays on one host; therefore RK-23
   stands behind RK-13. There, however, it reaches beyond every granted permission, up to sessions
   and logins of other people.
5. **RK-07:** No fix takes back knowledge that has reached another audience group. The
   server does not see the memory of the runtime; the mitigation is a recommendation and
   hint H1, and no test in the repository reaches the remainder (T-AI-01, residual risk `medium`).
   It reaches across the boundary between audience groups, but only as knowledge. Therefore RK-07
   stands behind RK-23, whose remainder allows acting.

**Behind rank 5** are all other risks with the rating `medium` and `high` or `high`
and `medium`:

- **RK-27** (open terminal sessions in the console) meets criteria 2 and 3 like RK-07
  (T-TM-07, residual risk `medium`), but its remainder reaches less far. It requires an already
  compromised browser of a person who may open the terminal, and stays within that person's
  permissions; that is the same remainder as with malware on any device (T-TK-01). The remainder of
  RK-07, by contrast, goes across the boundary between audience groups.
- **RK-02, RK-04, RK-33, RK-49 and RK-50** meet criterion 2: content with people without
  read permission, requests into internal networks, deleted data that comes back (legal violation),
  content in the telemetry, data loss after a faulty backup. By criterion 3 less
  remains: their cause lies in code and configuration of the repository, and tests cover it.
  RK-02 is checked by the differential test AZ-P8 and the case tables per action (T-AZ-03), RK-04 by
  the egress client with address check after resolution and SSRF lint (T-NW-01), RK-33 by the
  deletion journal, the startup check and the tests SEC-T15 and DP-T07 (T-BK-02), RK-49 by the
  allow-list in the gate, the linter for log messages and the canary test (T-LG-01), RK-50 by the
  integration test of archiving, backup and restore (ADR 0058). The named threats are listed
  in the threat model with the residual risk `low`. RK-50 has no entry there; by
  criterion 3 its remainder is `low` for the same reason.
- **RK-11, RK-12, RK-14, RK-15, RK-31, RK-39, RK-44, RK-56 and RK-85** do not meet criterion 2.
  Their consequence is a restriction (RK-11, RK-39, RK-44), a break at an interface (RK-12,
  RK-14, RK-15), a limit of throughput (RK-31), effort (RK-56) or a skipped
  check step (RK-85); a later fix remedies them.
