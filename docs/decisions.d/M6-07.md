# M6-07 Permission hints and expiry overview

| No | Date | Ruling | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-M6-07-1 | 2026-10-22 | One H1 function: `domain.ReadHorizonGaps(agent, H(A) paths, T(A), snapshots)` returns one hint per person of T(A) and channel of H(A) the person cannot read. M1-49 computed the gaps inline in `app/explain.go`; the explainer now calls the domain function, and the list "Permission hints" calls the same explainer method (`readHorizon`), so "who has access" (`via_agent`) and the list never disagree. H(A) and T(A) stay `domain.Horizon` and `domain.Triggerers` of M1-49 (T(A) without the people SR2 blocks). | Brief Design note 1; 07 section 6. |
| D-M6-07-2 | 2026-10-22 | The other three kinds. `grant_to_disabled`: a grant in effect whose subject person, agent or app has status `disabled` or `deleted`; a pending person's grants take effect (07 SR7) and do not count, a group never (deleting a group revokes its grants). `trigger_blocked`: a grant in effect with level `trigger` to an active person or a group that reaches at least one channel with an effective operator (archived channels left out, no one triggers there), where on every such channel SR2 (operator of effective tool class `host`) or SR3 (project binding) blocks the subject exactly as the guard of `agent.invoke` does: no owner and no ready own dev environment (for a group: no member in effect with one); the hint names the blocking rules (`sr2`, `sr3`). A disabled subject is reported as `grant_to_disabled` only. A trigger grant that reaches no operated channel gives no hint, because SR2 and SR3 are not the reason. `agent_personal`: every active agent in mode `personal` with its credential holder and its trigger set of SR1 (`domain.TriggerSet`, the set I1 checks). | Brief Design note 1; 07 sections 9.5, SR2, SR3. |
| D-M6-07-3 | 2026-10-22 | Scope and paging. The instance needs the owner role; a space needs owner or `manage` there (`authorize(access.hints.read)` on `instance` or `space`; no read `404`, read without `manage` `403`; a space that does not exist is `404` for owners too). For a space, H1 covers the channels of the space for every agent, not only the agents that operate a channel of the space: an agent of another space that reads a channel of this space can leak it to its own triggering people, and that is exactly what the manager of this space must see. The grant kinds cover grants on the space and its channels; `agent_personal` the personal agents that serve a target in the space. Every agent comes from the new source port `authz.AgentDirectory` (agents module), because an agent can serve a channel only through the threads it operates. The list is ordered by kind (`h1_read_horizon`, `grant_to_disabled`, `trigger_blocked`, `agent_personal`) and within a kind by agent, person and channel, by grant ID or by agent ID; the cursor carries the kind and the key, and a page is computed lazily (H1 agent by agent), so every hint appears on exactly one page. Measured with 50 agents and 500 people: the whole H1 list in one request about 1.7 s on a developer machine, a first page of three about 0.3 s, both within `http.request_timeout`. | Brief Design note 2; 07 section 2.6. |
| D-M6-07-4 | 2026-10-22 | "Expiring soon": grants not revoked and memberships not removed (of live groups) with `expires_at` in `(now, now + authz.expiry_reminder_lead]`, whether reminded or not (the reminder marker belongs to the job of M1-30, the list shows the state); tokens of agents and apps not revoked in the same window from the new source port `authz.TokenExpirySource` (identity, ids and expiry only), only on the instance and so only for owners (R-AZ-34); never personal tokens. Ordered by expiry, then grant, membership, token, then ID, paged by that position. For a space: grants on the space and its channels and the memberships of groups that hold a grant in effect on such a target. A page carries the names of its principals, groups, spaces and channels (`AccessLabels`, also on the hints), so the client builds its sentences without further reads and no name beyond the administration lists appears. | Brief Design note 3; 07 section 9.4. |
| D-M6-07-5 | 2026-10-22 | "Extend" creates the new grant (subject, target, level and capabilities of the old one, a new reason, a new expiry) and then revokes the old one, both through the step-up interceptor, so one step-up within `auth.step_up_max_age` covers both and the access never lapses in between; a failed creation revokes nothing. A token is renewed by creating a new one (secret shown once) and revoking the old one in the token screen of the agent. | Brief Design note 4; 07 sections 2.5 and 9.4. |
| D-M6-07-6 | 2026-10-22 | Names of the merged code instead of the brief (D-W-100): the port `identity.TokenExpiry` of the brief is the source port `authz.TokenExpirySource` declared by authz (02 section 4.1 rule 4) and implemented by `identity/app.TokenExpiry`; `chat.Operators` needs no new port, the effective operator per channel comes from `StructureSource.Channels` as in M1-49; the cursor codec of M1-18 is `app.Cursors`. The hint field of the blocking rule is the list `rules`, because SR2 and SR3 can block the same grant on different channels. | Merged names win (D-W-100). |
