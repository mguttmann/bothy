# AUD-srv-notify-01 Push registrations survive outages, a disabled row keeps its reason, cheaper badge reads

Work package of the audit of 2026-11-11 for the module `notify`, findings srv-notify-1,
srv-notify-10, performance-5, srv-notify-2, performance-7, srv-notify-11 and
fn-push-notifications-8.

| No | Date | Ruling | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| AUD-SN01/1 | 2026-11-12 | A transient failure of a push (the result class `retry`: a `5xx` of the provider, or no answer at all, which the job counts as `retry`) no longer deactivates a registration. `push.max_consecutive_failures` gets the default `0` and the lower bound `0`, and `0` means that no number of such failures deactivates; `domain.ApplyResult` still raises `failure_count` (it stops at the largest value of the column), an accepted push still sets it back to `0`, and the hint is retried with the backoff of 13 7.3 until `push.max_age`. A value above `0` keeps the rule of D-M3-19-12 unchanged: at that many transient failures in a row the row gets `repeated_failures` and stays disabled like every other disabled row. Amends D-M3-19-12 in the default only. Not built: a circuit breaker per provider, a check of the registration by the app at its start, an alert on `repeated_failures`, and a migration that enables rows an earlier version disabled with `repeated_failures` (the next registration of the app replaces such a row, D-M3-22-4). | Finding srv-notify-1: the class `retry` holds only answers that say nothing about the token, and the first retries of one hint follow each other within a few `push.retry_base`, so one short outage of the provider, of name resolution or of the egress path reached the limit and ended push for the device; nothing enables a disabled row again, and the app registers only for a new token or a new login. With the default the cause is gone, so neither healing nor an alert is needed; the adapters already pause on configuration errors, and backoff plus `push.max_age` bound the load of an outage. The key stays, with its meaning for every value an instance could set before, because a key is never removed or reinterpreted without a major version (configuration.md section 10 rule 4); an instance that sets it above `0` chooses the old behaviour with its cost. Tests: `TestPushSend_OutageKeepsRegistration` (the job through the real FCM adapter against Fake-FCM, red before with the row `repeated_failures` at the fifth attempt), `TestApplyResult_NoLimit`, `TestSend_NoLimitKeepsRegistration`, `TestNewPush_FailureLimit`. |
| AUD-SN01/2 | 2026-11-12 | `domain.ApplyResult` leaves a row that is already disabled exactly as it is, whatever the class of the answer: no write, no retry of the hint; `bothy.outcome` still names the answer, so `bothy.push.sent` counts the request that happened. | Finding srv-notify-10, first part, verified in the code: step 3 of `notify.push_send` locked the row and applied the answer without looking at `disabled_at`, so a session end, a revocation or a newer registration between step 1 and step 3 lost its reason and its time to a late `410` (`token_invalid`) or a counted failure, against 13 7.3 ("a row that is already disabled keeps its first reason"); the moved `disabled_at` restarted the retention period of the row, and a `retry` or `throttled` answer inserted another attempt for a row no job may send to. Tests: `TestApplyResult_DisabledRowStays`, `TestSend_DisabledDuringTheCall` (both red before). |
| AUD-SN01/3 | 2026-11-12 | The previous read position of a `read_state.updated` is read as one event: the new method `sync.History.LastPersonalEvent` (query `LastPersonalEvent`: audience, resource, types, `seq` below a bound, `ORDER BY seq DESC LIMIT 1`) returns the newest earlier read of that person in that thread, and `Push.previousPosition` uses it instead of `ResourceEvents`. The rule of 13 8 is unchanged (the newest earlier `read_state.updated` of the person for the thread, else 0). Not built: reads in the load test `perf/push_handoff`. | Finding performance-5: `ResourceEvents` returned every retained `read_state.updated` of the thread, of every reader and with its payload, for each read the consumer `push` handles, and the person's own event was searched in memory; that grows with readers times reads within `sync.event_retention`. The read belongs to `sync`, which alone reads `event_log` (03 principle 12), so the port grows by one method; the query is exempt from the visibility predicate like the two other history reads (`tools/lint/sql_visibility.yaml`). Tests: `TestHistory_LastPersonalEvent` (PostgreSQL, many readers of one thread), `TestReadState_PreviousPositionIsOneEvent` (red before: the consumer loaded the reads of every person). |
| AUD-SN01/4 | 2026-11-12 | One badge count no longer reads the place of every unread thread. `Facts.LevelFactsOfUser` takes the threads with their channel (`chat.ThreadPosition`, as `UnreadCandidates` and `Info` return them) and reads the notification path once per channel; the threads of a channel that no longer exists are left out, as a missing thread was before. `Push.counting` runs its `push.deliver` checks under one request memo and one structure memo (`badgeScope`), so one count reads the rights version once and the facts of a channel once instead of once per counted thread; a count inside the request of the preview keeps that request's memo. What stays, by 13 8 and D-M3-19-15: the badge is counted for every job, retry and preview at that moment, every counted thread is confirmed with its own `push.deliver` check, and the reads of the candidate threads and of the read positions return a row per thread of the visible channels. That remainder is what performance-7 names, and it is deferred: bounding the rows of one count needs a number kept or cached per person, a new design in place of D-M3-19-15, and a load test with a stock of threads to measure it; none of the three is built here. | Findings srv-notify-2 (the reads per thread, fixed by this) and performance-7 (the count grows with the threads of the visible channels; the same code, its queries per thread are gone with this, the rest is deferred). Verified: up to three reads (thread, channel, space) per unread thread without a mention outside a DM channel, in the transaction of step 1 of every push job, and a read of the rights version and of the channel per counted thread. The channel of a thread is already in the candidate row, and the level facts of a thread differ from those of its channel only by the person's own row of the thread, which is matched in memory. Joining the candidates with the read positions in SQL crosses the modules `chat` and `sync` (07 7.4 rule 6). A cached number would be stale right after a read, exactly when the badge sync sends it; a kept number needs a table that follows messages, reads, levels and rights. No damage is measured, and `perf/push_handoff` runs in a world without a stock of threads. Tests: `TestLevelFactsOfUser_OnePathReadPerChannel` (red before with one path read per thread), `TestBadge_OneScopePerCount` (red before without a memo); `TestBadgeCount` and `TestBadgeSync` hold the numbers over the real modules. |
| AUD-SN01/5 | 2026-11-12 | No change for the second part of srv-notify-10: a registration that a newer token of the same app installation replaced keeps `disabled_reason = 'token_invalid'` (D-M3-22-4), and `PushStats` counts it like a token the provider refused. The README of `notify` now says so. | A reason of its own changes the check of `push_registrations.disabled_reason` in the core contract (03), needs a migration and changes the published schema of the data export. Without it a replaced row can be told from a refused one only while the row that replaced it exists (the same device, provider and app, created at the `disabled_at` of the replaced row), and a takeover or `unregisterPush` deletes that row, so a count that leaves replaced rows out would change afterwards. The reason was chosen with the replacement (D-M3-22-4), and no reader other than the rate `push_failure_rate_<provider>` tells the reasons apart. |
| AUD-SN01/6 | 2026-11-12 | No change, finding srv-notify-11: a session that only expires keeps its registrations until the retention removes the session (D-M3-22-7, 13 7.3). | The facts hold: only `identity.SessionEnded` and `identity.DeviceRevoked` deactivate, and step 1 of `notify.push_send` checks the person, the device and `push.deliver`, not the session. It is the ruling of M3-22, and the other side costs more: every app session ends at `auth.session_max_age` (08, "new login afterwards"), so a check of the session in the send step would end every push of a person from that moment until the app is opened again, with nothing on the device that says so, while today the hint still arrives and leads back to the login. Such a push carries the kind, IDs and the badge number, no name and no text, and the preview of an expired session answers `401 session_revoked` (13 6.4). A lost device is ended with `device.revoke`, which disables its registrations in the same transaction, and a device without contact is revoked after `auth.device_inactive_revoke_after`. A change would be a read of the session's refresh family in step 1 through a new port of `identity`. |
| AUD-SN01/7 | 2026-11-12 | Deferred, finding fn-push-notifications-8: a hint routed to a connection that ended a moment before is lost, and no push follows (at most once, D-M3-23-3; AUD-CA1/2 names the same limit from the phone). Open question for the owner: should the kinds a person must not miss (`mention`, `dm`, `input_needed`, `run_done`, `run_failed`) get a push when no client confirmed the gateway hint in time? | The facts hold on the current code: `GatewaySend` publishes once and has no fallback, a worker sees the end of a connection only with the next `cluster.presence_flush_interval`, a connection that broke without a close counts until the heartbeat limit, and `apps/client/e2e_test/phone/push_e2e_test.dart` lets Sam write again for that reason. The remedy is a feature, not a correction of this module: an acknowledgement of `notification.created` with its `nid` (a new op of the gateway contract and of every client), a job that sends the push of the hint when none arrived by a deadline (a new configuration key, and a rule for a device that showed the hint and then got the push too), and a presence row that ends with its connection (`gateway`). Until then the end-to-end tests keep their repetition. |
