# Fix: one device follows the navigation of another

| No | Date | Ruling | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| FIX-DM/1 | 2026-10-29 | Navigation state is per device and is never personal data of 04 14: no event, no setting key and no REST operation carries the route, the selected sidebar row, the open or hidden columns or a scroll position (01 6.7 R-PR-24, 04 14 R-SY-29). | Owner report of 2026-10-29: with one person signed in on an iPhone and on the Mac app, opening a chat on the phone appeared to open the same chat on the Mac. The owner wants to do different things on each device; only data may sync. Neither chapter had a rule either way, so the rule is written down. |
| FIX-DM/2 | 2026-10-29 | The client keeps its current shape: the route lives in the router of the app process, the selection in `lastRouteSelectionProvider` (memory), the column and group choices in the local database (`forum_view_states`, `channel_view_states`, `shell_view_state`), the recent targets in `recent_targets`. Superseded in part by FIX-DM2/1: the collapse of a space was synchronized and now stays on the device as well. | Searched before the fix: the router changes only on an action of the person on that device (a tap or keyboard shortcut in the app, a clicked desktop notification, a tapped push, a deep link, a share on Android). No sync applier, provider or event of 04 14 writes a route or a selection, and the settings keys the client writes are `appearance.*`, `composer.enter_sends`, `privacy.*` and `layout.column_widths` (widths, not which column is open). |
| FIX-DM/3 | 2026-10-29 | The reproduction is two client tests over the fake server (`apps/client/test/app/device_navigation_test.dart`): device A runs the desktop app on `support`; device B opens "Release notes" as its history column does (focus, thread read, read position) while Sam writes there, and, in the second test, B runs the phone app and opens the thread through its router. A keeps its route, its selection is empty and it shows no history column of that thread, while the read state of B reaches A. Both tests pass on the code before this change. | The route did not follow against the fake server, so these tests are a guard of the rule; the mirroring the owner saw was the collapse of a space (FIX-DM2/1, reproduced in `space_state_device_test.dart`). What they do not cover: the real server (only the fake gateway), the native notification and push glue of iOS and macOS (a click on a desktop notification or push opens its thread by design, 01 10.3), and a build older than the current `main`. If the owner sees it again, the next step is the route log of the Mac app at that moment (which source called the router: notification, push or deep link). |
