# AUD-client-app-01 Hints on a phone in front, the lock and assistive technology, the permission moment and the column borders

| No | Date | Ruling | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| AUD-CA1/1 | 2026-11-12 | A phone that is in front shows `notification.created` as a banner inside the app. The presenter of a phone build is `InAppBanners` (`inAppBannersProvider`, chosen by the form factor of the build in the provider, not in the ports: a banner is a part of the app's own screen, and so the app widget of a test takes the same path as a release build); it stands behind the same `NotificationRouter` as the local notifications of the desktop, so the rules are the same: nothing for the thread that is on screen, the title and the text of 01 10.2, `security` opens the devices settings and `admin` the administration. `InAppBannerHost` lays the banner over the content at the top inside the safe area, in the builder of the app widget, so it also shows over a dialog or a sheet. One banner shows at a time and a later hint replaces it, whatever its slot. It leaves by itself after `InAppBannerHost.stay`; while a screen reader or a switch control is active (`MediaQuery.accessibleNavigation`) it stays until the person opens or dismisses it or the next hint replaces it. A tap opens the target as a tapped push does (the router with `ExternalLink`), the close button only removes it. It appears without an animation and is announced once as a live region. No banner shows while the app is signed out or the app lock is closed (the step the router's redirect follows); a hint that arrived behind the lock shows after the unlock, and the banner of an account goes with its engine. Nothing of a banner is stored. | Audit finding fn-push-notifications-4: 13 4.6 item 1 gives a connected device the gateway event instead of a push and says "the app shows its own notification or an in-app banner", and the server gives every connected device only that route, also a phone. The phone build had the presenter that shows nothing, so a mention, a question of a run and the hint about a waiting device only moved a counter, and no push followed, because the hint counted as delivered. `apps/client/test/notifications/in_app_banner_flow_test.dart` runs the phone app of an iOS and an Android build over the real services against the fake server: the hint arrives as a frame of the gateway, shows as a banner, and a tap opens the thread at the message. `apps/client/integration_test/phone/in_app_banner_test.dart` runs the same on the simulator and the emulator in the phone suite. |
| AUD-CA1/2 | 2026-11-12 | The banner is no notification of the system and has no sound. A hint that is not quiet gives one haptic tap when its banner first shows (`HapticFeedback.mediumImpact`); a quiet hint (quiet hours, 13 3.4) gives none. Not built: a local notification of iOS or Android for the event, and a push that follows when the person locks the phone after the banner left. | The spec allows either form, and the phone is in front when the event arrives. A local notification would have to share the slots of the native push path (the tag and group of a thread, the `cleared` list of a sync push, the badge of 13 8), which live in the runners without a Flutter engine (ADR 0056), or two kinds of notifications of one thread would stand side by side; that is a larger change across both runners. A sound needs an audio file and a player in the app; the finding names the missing sound, its recommendation asks for the banner or a local notification, and the haptic tap stands in for the sound. What this does not cover: the event is delivered at most once (13 4.6, D-M3-23-3), so a hint that arrives while the phone lies unattended in front leaves only its counter once the banner's time is over, and the server sends no push for it later; changing that is a decision about the server's routes, outside this component. |
| AUD-CA1/3 | 2026-11-12 | For the app lock, what a person does through a screen reader or a switch control is activity like a pointer or key event: every semantics action the platform sends for a node (`SemanticsBinding.addSemanticsActionListener`: a double tap, a swipe that scrolls, increase and decrease) and every move of the screen reader's focus to another node (`ServicesBinding.accessibilityFocus`, which the runners of iOS and Android report for every node, also plain text). This refines D-M5-08-2. An incoming message, an upload or a policy change stays no activity. | Audit finding a11y-4: VoiceOver and TalkBack act through semantics actions and send no pointer event, so a person who read a long thread with a screen reader was locked out after `client.app_lock_after` in the middle of using the app. `apps/client/test/lock/lock_activity_test.dart` sends a focus move and a double tap the way the engine delivers them and holds the lock open. Not covered: an assistive tool that neither moves the focus of the system nor sends an action nor a key (none is known). |
| AUD-CA1/4 | 2026-11-12 | The app asks for the notification permission when the person first opens a history after the login: a thread under `/t/<id>` as before, and also the main history a text channel shows under `/c/<id>`. The post list of a forum, the new chat view and the start page ask nothing. The app widget reads the channel of the route from the local database; a channel that is not stored yet asks nothing, and the next history asks. This refines D-M5-04-5 and D-M5-05-6 ("when the person first opens a thread"): once per run and never before the login stay. | Audit finding client-phone-background-5: the question was bound to the path prefix `/t/`, so a person who only read text channels was never asked, registered no token and got no push, without any hint in the settings. The main history of a text channel is a thread (`kind = main`), and opening it is as good a moment to explain the question as any other thread. `apps/client/test/notifications/push_permission_moment_test.dart`. |
| AUD-CA1/5 | 2026-11-12 | A column border of the desktop (`ColumnDragHandle`) moves without a pointer. It takes the keyboard focus in the order of the columns (a stop between its two columns); Arrow left and Arrow right move it by `ColumnDragHandle.step` per key press and repeat, and the width is written once when the key is released or the focus leaves. A key with Ctrl, Alt or Cmd is not the border's. For a screen reader the border is a slider named "Resize column" with the width of its column as the value; "increase" and "decrease" make that column wider and narrower by the same step, each as one write, also for the session column, whose border lies before it. The bounds of a drag hold (`DsLayout.columnMin`, the room of the window). No menu entry and no shortcut for the widths is added; "Reset" stays in the settings. | Audit finding a11y-8, verified at the source: the border was a 4 pixel strip with a horizontal drag only, no focus and no key, and its semantics node had a name but neither a role nor an action, so the keyboard could not change a width at all (WCAG 2.1.1) and a screen reader stopped at a node it could do nothing with. 01 8 sets WCAG 2.2 AA as the goal. A focusable separator with arrow keys is the pattern of a window splitter; it needs no new text. `apps/client/test/app/column_widths_test.dart`. |
| AUD-CA1/6 | 2026-11-12 | Deferred: a local diagnostic log for release builds (audit finding client-shell-3: no log target, `logHook` of `auth` and `sync_engine` not wired, no global error handler). Nothing is built here. | The facts hold on the current `main`: every log line of the app goes to `developer.log`, which a release build keeps nowhere, no code sets a `logHook`, and `main.dart` installs no error handler. FIX-WK/3 already names the gap and why it is not a small fix: a log file is a new store with its own retention and content rules. The recommended fix is a feature of its own: a port with a bounded, rotating file, rules for what a line may contain (the log lines of the app carry no content and no token today, as 13 10 and 17 4 ask of telemetry), the wiring of every sink, global error handlers that write to it, and an export in the settings on four platforms with its texts and tests. Global handlers alone would have nowhere to write. Question for the owner: shall release builds keep such a log on the device, with which retention, and may a person export and send it (a product and privacy decision)? Until then crash reports with a build target (D-M4-31-3, D-M4-31-5) are the only diagnosis of a release build. |

The checklist of the phones says so now: the row "High contrast" of the manual pass is marked as not supported, and what the pass still checks with the setting on. | Verified at the source: the app widget passes one theme and no high-contrast theme, and no interface package reads `MediaQuery.highContrast`.
