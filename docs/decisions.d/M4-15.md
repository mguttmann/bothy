| D-M4-15-1 | 2026-10-16 | Outcome classes: a return of the executor is `confirmed` (`2xx`, `200` with `Idempotent-Replay: true`, `204`). | D-M4-15-3 | 2026-10-16 | One worker per FIFO lane; the lane is the thread (`thread:<id>`), or what the executor names for writes outside a thread (`setting:<key>` for own settings, `message:<id>` for reactions without a thread, `upload:<id>` for M4-22), else `global`. Lanes run in parallel; the HTTP client's connection limit per host bounds them, and the outbox has no number of its own. Only the oldest entry that is not final in a lane may go; a failed entry is skipped (it waits for the person), a dependency that is not confirmed blocks the lane. | Design note 4 of the brief; 04 13.2 item 2. |

| D-M4-15-5 | 2026-10-16 | "Not sent yet" is `attempts == 0`. Only such an entry is edited or removed in place. An attempted entry (`sending`, `waiting`, or `pending` again after a restart) keeps its ID and payload; an edit queues a follow-up `updateMessage` and a removal a follow-up `deleteMessage` behind the newest entry of its follow-up chain, and a second edit changes a follow-up that has not been sent yet in place. An edit holds the changed members and is merged into the stored payload, so `message_id` and the slots the outbox fills stay. A failed entry edited or sent again becomes a new entry with a new ID; after a `412` the new entry carries the `version` of `conflict_current` as `If-Match`. All changes by the person and every step of the sender run under one lock, so an edit never changes a payload that is being sent. | Design note 6 of the brief; 04 13.3 and 04 16.2 (a sent entry may be stored on the server). |
| D-M4-15-6 | 2026-10-16 | `depends_on` holds the IDs an entry waits for, comma-separated (a message waits for two uploads; UUIDs contain no comma); the outbox selects the lanes itself and does not use `OutboxDao.nextForThread`, which reads the column as one ID. A confirmed entry leaves the table, so a missing dependency counts as confirmed; discarding an entry first fills the members its dependents hold as null from its own non-null members (a discarded follow-up edit hands the server's `message_id` to the delete behind it) and fails them with `dependency_discarded`, and replacing a failed entry with a new ID (`retry`, `edit`) re-points every entry that waits for it to the replacement through the new `OutboxDao.updateDependsOn`, so nothing is sent without what it needs. Within a lane a dependent never goes before its parent, also when a clock step gave it the older `created_at`. On confirmation the executor's `result` (for a message `message_id` and `version`) fills the payload members a dependent holds as null; a follow-up gets the server's ID of the message it edits this way. | The schema of M4-12 has one `depends_on` column and no update of it; the brief needs several dependencies and follow-ups of entries whose server ID is not known yet. |
| D-M4-15-7 | 2026-10-16 | "Sending delayed": an entry in `waiting` reports `delayed` once `client.outbox_stuck_after` has passed since it was written (`created_at`), not since its last state change; `watchThread` publishes again at that moment from the injected timer, without a database change. | 15 7.5; a backoff resets the state time on every attempt, so the state time would never reach the limit while the person waits. |
| D-M4-15-8 | 2026-10-16 | `packages/local_store` gains one method, `OutboxDao.updateDependsOn(id, dependsOn)`, with its test; no column and no migration change. | D-M4-15-9 | 2026-10-16 | The sender picks and claims the head of every idle lane in one section under the lock, on fresh data, so a change by the person or the outcome of another request never slips between the choice and the claim (dependencies and `next_attempt_at` hold). `start()` marks the outbox started before the first reset of `sending` entries and schedules only after it, so a `pause()` at any time holds. An edit of a failed entry drops the never sent follow-up edits behind it (they carry older texts); the entries behind them wait for the new entry. Discarding an entry fails its whole chain, not only the direct dependents, so no entry waits for a failed one and blocks its lane. Every dependency the outbox writes itself (the replacement of `retry` and `edit`, a follow-up of `edit` and `remove`, the bridge over a dropped follow-up) skips an entry that failed with its chain (`dependency_discarded`) and waits for what that entry waited for instead; an entry that failed for its own reason stays a dependency until it is retried or discarded; dependencies named in `enqueue` stay, so a message keeps waiting for its failed upload until the person retries or discards it. Reaction emoji are percent-encoded before they go into the path. | Findings of the red team: a stale snapshot sent a message before its new upload and ignored a `Retry-After`; a pause during the first start was lost; an older follow-up overwrote the newest edit; a discarded create left its delete blocking the thread, and a retried delete waited for its chain-failed edit; `#` and `?` emoji ended the path. |
| D-M4-15-10 | 2026-10-16 | One resolver replaces the case patches of the dependency rule (`lib/src/outbox/dependencies.dart`, owner decision 2026-10-16). Every row that leaves the table goes through it and re-points every entry that names it: a confirmed entry is dropped from the lists, a retried or edited one becomes its replacement, a dropped follow-up becomes what it waited for, a discarded one is dropped after its chain failed with `dependency_discarded`.
